# Lecture 10: Kalman Filtering (KF & EKF)

> **The hook:** Your model says the robot is here. Your sensor says it is there. The Kalman filter is the optimal compromise — when the noise is Gaussian.

<Note title="What We Cover">

- State, covariance, and the Bayes filter
- Linear KF: predict and update
- Kalman gain as a trust ratio
- EKF: nonlinear models, linearized covariance
- Innovation and filter consistency (NIS)

</Note>

---

## Predict

Push the state through the motion model and grow the covariance. In the EKF, the mean goes through the true nonlinear `f`; the covariance goes through its Jacobian `F`.

```python
x_pred = f(x, u)
F = jacobian_f(x, u)
Sigma_pred = F @ Sigma @ F.T + Q
```

---

## Update

Linearize the measurement model, form the innovation covariance `S`, and compute the gain. `K` is large when the prediction is uncertain and the sensor is precise, small in the opposite case.

```python
H = jacobian_h(x_pred)
S = H @ Sigma_pred @ H.T + R
K = Sigma_pred @ H.T @ np.linalg.inv(S)
y = z - h(x_pred)
x = x_pred + K @ y
Sigma = (np.eye(n) - K @ H) @ Sigma_pred
```

---

## Is the Filter Honest?

The normalized innovation squared `y^T S^-1 y` should follow a χ² distribution. If it is consistently too large, the filter is overconfident — `Q` or `R` is too small.

<Warning title="Common Misconception">

Predicting the state with `F @ x` in the EKF (discarding the nonlinearity) or reversing the gain product order. Mean through `f`, covariance through `F`.

</Warning>
